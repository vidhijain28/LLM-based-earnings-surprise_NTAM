Groundwork for joining each Capital IQ earnings-call transcript (ciq_transcripts) to its I/B/E/S earnings announcement on WRDS: the reported EPS, the analyst consensus before the release, the announcement timestamp, and the earnings surprise (SUE). The surprise is the benchmark the LLM signal has to beat (Koijen & Levy use it as the baseline regressor). This notebook works out how to do the join and how well it works. The production script comes next.

Questions:

  1. What does I/B/E/S on WRDS contain, which tables do we need, and how current is it?
  2. How do we get from a CIQ companyid to an IBES ticker? (CIQ has no IBES identifier, so we compare four indirect routes.)
  3. How do we match a call to an IBES announcement, and what do we do about ties and duplicates?
  4. How good is the match: by year, US vs non-US, region, and why do calls go unmatched?
  5. What does the matched record give us: actual, consensus, number of analysts, consensus age, SUE? How does the monthly summary consensus compare with one rebuilt from analyst-level estimates?
  6. Timing: when is the release relative to the call, and what is the event date under the paper's 16:00 ET rule?
  7. A worked example (Apple).

As in the other notebooks, heavy filtering runs on the WRDS server. We pull only quarterly / semi-annual / annual EPS rows, which are a few million rows.

1. What is in I/B/E/S on WRDS?¶

I/B/E/S (LSEG, formerly Thomson Reuters) is split into a US file (*_epsus, US-domiciled firms) and an international file (*_epsint). Each comes in an adjusted version (act_, statsum_, det_; per-share values restated for later splits) and an unadjusted version (actu_, statsumu_, detu_; values as of the date). We use the unadjusted tables. The adjusted ones are rounded to the cent after split-adjustment, which distorts small surprises (Payne & Thomas 2003), and the unadjusted actual, consensus and price are all on the same per-share basis on the announcement date.

2. From CIQ companyid to IBES ticker¶

CIQ on WRDS carries no IBES identifier: the symbol type TF Ibes Ticker (id 864) exists in ciq.ciqsymboltype, but there are no rows for it in ciq.ciqsymbol. So we have to go through other identifiers. We try four routes, each evaluated as of the call date where the link tables have date ranges:

Route Path Notes
A. CUSIP ciq.wrds_cusip (9-char, dated) -> first 8 chars = ibes.id.cusip IBES cusip is a real CUSIP only for US/Canadian listings
B. CCM ciq.wrds_gvkey -> crsp.ccmxpf_lnkhist (LU/LC, P/C) -> permno -> ibcrsphist US-listed (CRSP) securities only
C. Compustat ibtic gvkey -> comp.security / comp.g_security.ibtic Compustat's own IBES ticker per issue, North America and Global; not dated

  • Non-US calls: CUSIP (45%) and CCM (20%) are weak, since they only cover North American CUSIPs and US-listed ADRs. Compustat ibtic (85%) and the SEDOL route (76%) do the work.   • Agreement is high: when two routes both return tickers, they share one 94-98% of the time. CUSIP vs ibtic agrees least (94%). A plausible cause is one route picking a US ADR line and the other the home listing; both are valid IBES entities, and the tie-break below handles that case.
  • 18% of linked calls have more than one candidate ticker (ADR vs home listing, share classes, ticker re-use after mergers). So we never trust a single ticker blindly: the announcement date decides which candidate is the right one.

3. Matching a call to an IBES announcement¶

An IBES "announcement" is a (ticker, anndats) pair. A firm usually releases quarterly, semi-annual and annual actuals together (the Q4 release carries both the Q4 and the fiscal-year EPS), so we first match the call to an announcement date, then pick the record to use: quarterly if present, otherwise semi-annual, otherwise annual.

Below: for each call, the gap in days between the call date and the nearest IBES announcement of any candidate ticker, within +/-10 days.

3,854,620 actual-EPS records -> 2,806,332 IBES announcements (ticker x date)
gap -3 -2 -1 0 1 2 3 4 5
share of calls with an announcement within 10 days 0.07% 0.09% 0.46% 70.21% 25.50% 1.09% 1.01% 0.45% 0.27%

The match is sharp: 70% of calls are on the IBES announcement date and 25.5% the day after. That fits firms that release after the close and hold the call the next morning, or that release pre-market and call later the same day. Gaps of -1 and +2/+3 days are mostly time-zone effects: the CIQ date is in UTC, while IBES dates are local. Beyond +3 days the mass is small and flat, so those pairs are more likely different events (for example a separate investor day) than late calls.

Rule: accept an announcement with call_date - anndats in [-1, +3] days.

After that it prefers the strongest identifier route.

The examples are the same company with two keydevids a day apart, the duplicate CIQ events already flagged in the coverage notebook (1.4% of calls share a company and date). In the script, dedupe on (ticker, anndats) and keep the call with the smallest gap (then the longer transcript).

Sanity check: does the matched IBES period agree with the call?¶

The CIQ headline names the fiscal period ("Apple Inc., Q3 2026 Earnings Call, Jul 30, 2026"). We parse the period label and compare it with the periodicity of the matched IBES record. We also check the days from the matched period end (pends) to the call, which should look like a reporting lag (a few weeks).

  • Headline "Qn" calls map to quarterly IBES actuals 99.5% of the time. Half-year headlines ("H1/H2") map to semi-annual actuals 58% of the time and to quarterly ones 42% of the time (firms that report a Q2/Q4 quarter inside the half-year release). That is the expected pattern, so the match is landing on the right kind of period.
  • The matched period ends a median 36 days (quarterly) or 53 days (semi-annual) before the announcement, the usual reporting lag. Only 0.05% of matches have an implausible lag.
  • For calendar-quarter firms, the headline "Qn YYYY" equals the IBES quarter for 83%. The rest are mainly non-December fiscal years, whose period end still falls in a calendar-quarter month (e.g. a June fiscal year: "Q1 2025" ends Sep 2024). So the remaining mismatch is a labeling difference, not a wrong match. In the script, pends from IBES (not the headline) should define the fiscal quarter.

5. What the match gives us: actual, consensus, surprise¶

For each matched quarterly announcement we attach the IBES summary consensus for that quarter (statsumu, fpi = '6', fpedats = pends), taken from the last monthly snapshot before the announcement, plus the IBES price at that snapshot. The standard surprise is

$$\text{SUE} = \frac{\text{actual} - \text{median consensus}}{\text{price}}$$

IBES actuals are "street" EPS (the same basis analysts forecast on, usually excluding one-offs), not GAAP, so actual and consensus are comparable.

  • US: 94% of matched calls get an actual, a consensus and a price, so the SUE is computable. The median consensus has 6 analysts, and 8% rest on a single estimate.
  • Non-US: only about 60% get a SUE. Semi-annual matches have no quarterly consensus, a quarter of the remaining consensuses rest on one analyst, and some international actuals are missing (value null). Currency mismatches between actual, consensus and price are rare (0.1-0.3%) and are dropped.
  • The summary consensus is a median 15 days old at the announcement (monthly snapshot; see below).
  • The beat/meet/miss split (60% / 7% / 33%) is the familiar pattern of firms beating the street.
  • SUE has huge tails (min -509x price) from penny stocks and near-zero prices. Following the paper (which ranks surprises within the quarter), use percentile ranks, or at least winsorize and require price >= $1.

Is the monthly summary consensus stale? Rebuild it from analyst-level estimates¶

statsumu is a snapshot taken once a month (the Thursday before the third Friday), so revisions made in the last weeks before the release are missing. For 2024 US quarterly announcements we rebuild the consensus on the server from detu_epsus: each analyst's latest estimate for that quarter made in the 90 days before the announcement date, then the median across analysts.

2024 US matched quarterly calls with both consensus versions: 9,998
  consensus differs: 68.3%; |difference| > 1 cent: 47.8%
  surprise sign differs (beat/meet/miss): 9.1%

  rank correlation of SUE, summary vs detail: 0.912
  days from latest individual estimate to announcement: median 21
  analysts: summary median 7, detail median 4
  • Rebuilding from analyst-level estimates changes the consensus for 68% of events (more than 1 cent for 48%) and flips the beat/meet/miss sign for 9%. The rank correlation of the two SUEs is still high (0.91).
  • The detail median uses fewer analysts (4 vs 7) because it keeps only estimates updated in the last 90 days, while IBES carries older un-stopped estimates in the summary. So this simple rebuild is fresher but noisier. A production version should also use the IBES stop file (stopu_epsus) and the actdats/revdats confirmation dates, rather than a fixed 90-day window.
  • For the benchmark regression, the cheap summary consensus is probably adequate (rho = 0.91). For a careful "surprise relative to what investors knew at the release" measure, the detail-based consensus is the better default. It is also the only option for a point-in-time consensus at an arbitrary timestamp.

6. Timing: release vs call, and the event date¶

IBES anntims is the time of the earnings release (US/Eastern for US firms). Koijen & Levy set the event date to the release day if it is a trading day and the news arrives by 16:00 ET, and to the next trading day otherwise. We apply that rule to US matched calls with a release time, using the CRSP trading calendar (available here through 2024).

  • IBES release times are real for practically every recent US announcement. The 00:00 placeholder is common only before 2000. Since 2015, US releases come either after the close (59%) or pre-market (40%), almost never during trading hours.
  • The call typically starts about 2 hours after the release (median 1.9h; 25% within 47 minutes). The long right tail (12-18h) is releases after the close with the call the next morning.
  • Under the 16:00 ET rule, the event date is the IBES announcement date for 41% of events and the next trading day for 59%.   • International anntims values cluster at 01:00-03:00, which looks like Eastern time (07:00-09:00 London), so they should be converted from ET too. Verify this before using intraday timing outside the US.

8. Takeaways and recipe for the join script¶

Bottom line: CIQ transcripts join to IBES cleanly. About 96% of US calls and about 85-90% of non-US calls (lower in recent years) find their IBES announcement, and for US calls about 94% also get a full SUE.

Recipe (what the script should do):

  1. Calls: one transcript per keydevid (Final, latest), with the date-aware primary gvkey. Dedupe CIQ duplicate events (same company, dates within a few days).
  2. Candidate IBES tickers, all evaluated as of the call date. Use the union of:
    • A CUSIP (ciq.wrds_cusip, first 8 chars) -> ibes.id.cusip
    • B gvkey -> CCM (LU/LC, P/C) -> permno -> ibcrsphist (open-ended last link)
    • C gvkey -> comp.security / comp.g_security.ibtic
    • D gvkey -> Compustat SEDOL (first 6 chars) -> IBES international cusip[2:8]
  3. Announcements: collapse actu_epsus + actu_epsint to (ticker, anndats), preferring the QTR record, then SAN, then ANN.
  4. Match: keep announcements with call_date - anndats in [-1, +3]. Pick the smallest |gap| (prefer +1 over -1), then the IBES file matching domicile, then the strongest route. Then dedupe so that each (ticker, anndats) keeps one call.
  5. Consensus: last statsumu snapshot (fpi = '6', fpedats = pends) before anndats, and/or a detail-based consensus from detu_* as of the release timestamp (respecting stopped estimates). Take the price from actpsumu at the same statpers (or from CRSP the day before the event). Drop currency mismatches.
  6. Output columns: keydevid, transcriptid, companyid, gvkey, ibes_ticker, link_route, ibes_file, pdicity, pends, anndats, anntims, event_date (16:00 ET rule), actual, consensus (median/mean), numest, dispersion, consensus_age, price, sue, match_gap_days, plus flags for ties and duplicates.

Caveats to carry forward:

  • IBES lags CIQ by about 7 weeks (monthly WRDS refresh). It is fine for a historical sample but not for the paper's live design.
  • The IBES-CRSP link table stops at 2026-01-31. Treat its open links as open-ended, or rely on the CUSIP route for recent US events.
  • Non-US: semi-annual reporters have no quarterly consensus for many calls, and international consensus is thin (a quarter of matches rest on one analyst). Decide early whether the sample is US-only (as in the paper) or global.
  • SUE scaling: use within-quarter percentile ranks (as in the paper) or winsorize, because price-scaled surprises have extreme tails.
  • Event date ≠ call date for about a third of US events. Use the IBES release timestamp with the 16:00 ET rule and a proper exchange calendar. CRSP's calendar here stops at 2024, so use exchange_calendars / pandas_market_calendars for later dates.
