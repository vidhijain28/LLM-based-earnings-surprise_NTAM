Basic look at the WRDS SEC Analytics Suite (wrdssec / wrds_sec_search schemas), the companion to the Capital IQ transcripts EDA. The suite is WRDS's processed copy of SEC EDGAR: every filing since 1994 (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, proxies, 13F, Forms 3/4/5, fund filings, ...), plus WRDS-built tables on top (8-K item codes, exhibit lists, CIK-gvkey links, Loughran-McDonald sentiment/readability, filing-to-filing text similarity) and the full filing text.

Questions:

  1. What tables exist, how big are they, which can we read, and how do they fit together?
  2. Which forms matter for us (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K), and how many are there?
  3. What is the grain of the filing index, and how do we keep one row per filing?
  4. How does coverage change over time?
  5. How well do filings link to Compustat gvkey?
  6. Timing: how fast after period end are 10-Ks/10-Qs filed, at what time of day, and how does that compare with the earnings announcement (8-K Item 2.02, Compustat rdq)?
  7. 8-K items: how often do firms file Item 2.02 (Results of Operations), and is the press release attached?
  8. What do the off-the-shelf text measures (LM sentiment, readability, cosine similarity) look like?
  9. What does the full text look like, and can we pull an MD&A section and an earnings press release out of it?

As in the transcripts notebook, all heavy aggregation runs on the WRDS server. The exhibit table is about 122M rows and the full-text tables are hundreds of GB, so we never pull them in full.

Keys: accession (e.g. 0000320193-26-000020) identifies one filing and joins everything. cik is the SEC filer ID, which links to Compustat gvkey through forms.gvkey or wciklink_gvkey.

2. Form types: what is in the index?¶

To answer the opening question directly: yes, the suite covers 10-Ks and 10-Qs, but also every other EDGAR form. For an earnings-surprise project the relevant ones are the periodic reports (10-K, 10-Q), the current report 8-K (the earnings press release is furnished under Item 2.02), their amendments, and the late-filing notices (NT 10-K/Q).

6. Timing: filing lag, time of day, and the earnings announcement¶

6a. Days from period end to filing¶

SEC deadlines depend on filer size: 10-Q within 40 days (large accelerated and accelerated filers) or 45 days (others); 10-K within 60 / 75 / 90 days.

count mean std min 10% 25% 50% 75% 90% max
ftype
10-K 69588.0 76.2 33.2 0.0 50.0 58.0 73.0 89.0 104.0 400.0
10-Q 189348.0 42.8 27.2 0.0 30.0 35.0 39.0 45.0 48.0 400.0

The deadlines are clearly visible. 10-Q median 39 days, with spikes exactly at 40 and 45 days. 10-K median 73 days, with spikes at 60, 75 and 90. The small secondary bumps around day 50 (10-Q) and day 105 (10-K) are firms using the 5-day / 15-day extension after filing an NT 10-Q / NT 10-K. Late filing is itself a known negative signal.

6b. Time of day of acceptance¶

secadate/secatime is EDGAR's acceptance timestamp (US/Eastern). Filings accepted after 17:30 ET get the next business day as their official filing date (fdate), so for event studies the acceptance timestamp is the one to use.

410,220 Item 2.02 8-Ks since 2005, 12,527 CIKs, 95.5% with gvkey

   10-K / 10-Q: pre-market (<9:30) 12%, market hours 30%, 16:00-17:30 49%, after 17:30 10% | fdate after acceptance date: 6.3%

 8-K Item 2.02: pre-market (<9:30) 35%, market hours 9%, 16:00-17:30 51%, after 17:30 5% | fdate after acceptance date: 3.3%

Earnings 8-Ks avoid market hours: about 35% are accepted before the open (mostly 6-9am ET) and about 51% between 16:00 and 17:30, with only 9% during the session. 10-K/10-Qs cluster just after the close too, but 30% arrive during market hours. About 6% of 10-K/10-Qs and 3% of earnings 8-Ks have an fdate later than their acceptance date (accepted after 17:30), so fdate alone can misplace the event by a day.

6c. Filings vs the earnings announcement date¶

8b. Filing-to-filing similarity (bow_cossim)¶

For each filing, bow_cossim compares the bag of words with the firm's earlier filings of the same form (lookback = 1, 2, 3, ... filings back). This is the input for "Lazy Prices"-style signals (Cohen, Malloy & Nguyen 2020): firms that change their 10-K/10-Q language tend to underperform.

Note: lookback counts filings, not quarters. Because Q4 is reported on the 10-K, the same 10-Q quarter one year earlier is usually lookback = 3, but gaps in filing history shift it. So we pick the comparison by the gap between periods of report instead.

9. Full text (wrds_sec_search)¶

Amendments sit in the same table (form = 10-K/A); older form variants such as 10-K405 sit in other tables. There is also a tsvector column with a RUM index, so keyword searches run on the server.

Full-text search on the server¶

The filing_tsv column supports Postgres full-text queries. Example: the share of all 8-Ks that mention tariffs, by quarter. This takes a few seconds per quarter.

Mentions of tariffs go from about 1% of 8-Ks before 2018, to about 4-5% during the 2018-2019 trade war, and to 15-18% in 2025-2026. Server-side keyword counts like this are cheap and work as a quick topic-exposure measure, or as a pre-filter before sending text to an LLM.

10. Takeaways for the earnings-surprise project¶

  • What the suite is: WRDS's processed EDGAR. Yes, 10-K and 10-Q, but for this project the most useful part is the 8-K Item 2.02 earnings press release (EX-99.1): about 17-19k a year since 2004, 98.5% with the press release attached, and full text in wrds_sec_search.filing_8_k.
  • Event timing: the 8-K acceptance timestamp (secadate + secatime, ET) falls on Compustat rdq 86% of the time and gives the intraday release time, mostly pre-open or 16:00-17:30. Use it, not fdate, which shifts after-17:30 filings to the next day. Together with the CIQ call timestamp, this gives a clean event clock: release, then call, then 10-Q.
  • 10-Q/10-K: filed a median 39 / 73 days after period end. When a press release exists, the 10-Q follows it 54% of the time (median +5 days). By 2024, though, about 59% of these quarters have the 10-Q filed on or before the release day. 10-Q/10-K text is better suited to a second-event or drift signal (MD&A tone, risk-factor changes, Lazy-Prices-style similarity) than to measuring the announcement surprise.
  • Linking: wrdssec.forms.gvkey covers 81% of 10-K/10-Q filings (87% recently). Most of the rest are ABS and commodity trusts outside Compustat. For a dated link use wciklink_gvkey, then CCM for permno and IBES for consensus.
  • Off-the-shelf text measures: wrds_nlpsa (LM tone and readability for every filing, current to date) and bow_cossim (similarity, through 2024) are convenient baselines. However, they are computed on the full submission including XBRL, so levels jump around 2011, and the item-level sec_item_scores is premium (no access). Plan to compute our own measures on the main document or EX-99.1 only.
  • Text for the LLM: full submissions are in wrds_sec_search (about 120 GB of 10-K, 198 GB of 10-Q and 150 GB of 8-K text), split by <DOCUMENT> tags. Keep the main document or EX-99.1 and drop the XBRL. Press releases are about 1-2k words and MD&As a few thousand, so both fit easily in a context window alongside the transcript. Use server-side tsvector search to pre-filter, and fetch text one accession at a time rather than in bulk.
